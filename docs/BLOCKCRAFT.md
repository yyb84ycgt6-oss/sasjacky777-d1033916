# BlockCraft

`/craft` (also `/minecraft`, and **Games & Worlds → ⛏️ BlockCraft** in the
sidebar) is a block-building survival game in the tradition of Minecraft:
survival, creative and hardcore; mining, crafting, smelting, farming; mobs,
water and lava, TNT, beds, day and night, weather; saves on the device, in a
file or in the cloud; the Nether and the End, with strongholds, the Ender
Dragon and elytra; 36 advancements to earn; and multiplayer online or
between tabs. It is an original
work — every texture, model and sound is generated by code in `src/craft`, so
there are no asset downloads, nothing licensed from Mojang, and the game works
offline once the page has loaded.

## Layout

```
src/craft/
  engine/   the world, independent of any screen: blocks, items, textures (painted
            procedurally), noise, worldgen, lighting, meshing, physics, mobs,
            block rules (fluids, crops, falling blocks), crafting, explosions.
            Runs in the browser, in workers and in vitest alike.
  render/   three.js: chunk meshes on a custom shader, sky, clouds, weather,
            particles, entity models, the first-person hand.
  game/     the running game: the 20-tick loop, actions (mining, placing, using),
            chunk streaming, input, commands, containers, saves, cloud saves.
  net/      multiplayer: transports (Supabase Realtime, BroadcastChannel) and
            the host/guest session.
  ui/       React over the canvas: title, worlds, HUD, inventories, menus,
            touch controls.
  audio.ts  synthesised sound effects and music.
src/pages/BlockCraft.tsx   the route
supabase/migrations/20260923120000_craft_worlds.sql   cloud saves
```

The game loop never goes through React. `Game` owns the world and the frame;
the UI reads a small store (`game/store.ts`) the game updates ten times a
second, plus on every inventory change.

## Two ways to play

**Desktop**: WASD, space, shift to sneak, ctrl or double-tap W to sprint, E
inventory, Q drop, T or / chat, 1–9 and the wheel for the hotbar, F1 hide HUD,
F2 screenshot, F3 debug, F5 camera, middle click to pick a block, double-tap
space to fly in creative. The mouse is captured while playing; every screen
releases it (see `src/test/craft-input.test.ts` for why each rule exists).

**Touch**: chosen automatically on touch devices (Options → Controls to
change). A joystick appears under the left thumb, dragging anywhere else looks
around, and buttons jump, sneak, mine and place — or "Tap to interact": tap to
place or use, hold to mine. Inventories have a quick-move toggle in place of
shift-click, and a long press is a right click.

## Redstone

`engine/redstone.ts` runs dust, torches, levers, buttons, pressure plates,
repeaters, comparators, lamps, pistons (sticky, with slime blocks dragging what
touches them), observers, daylight detectors, hoppers, droppers, dispensers,
iron doors and trapdoors, note blocks and TNT. It follows the original's rules
closely enough that circuits built from memory work: dust loses a level a block
and does not power more dust through the block it points into; a torch inverts
a redstone tick later and burns out if it is flicked more than eight times in
three seconds; a piston pushes at most twelve blocks and never obsidian, so a
slime block resting on the ground cannot be shoved sideways (it drags the
ground). Only the world's host runs it; guests see the result as block changes.

Slimes (swamps at night, and one chunk in ten below y 40 at any light) drop
the slimeballs sticky pistons and slime blocks need.

## Enchanting and brewing

`engine/enchanting.ts` holds twenty-three enchantments and the rules for
them. The table offers three enchantments, their level costs set by up to
fifteen bookshelves, and rolls them the original's way. The roll is seeded per
player, so the hint shown is what you get and reopening the table cannot
re-roll it. The anvil combines enchantments, repairs with materials or a second
copy, and renames; every job adds to the item's work penalty until it is "Too
Expensive!". An enchantment is data on the stack (`ench: { sharpness: 3 }`),
saved and sent over the network with it, and every one changes play: damage and
Smite/Bane against the undead and spiders, Protection points on top of armour,
Efficiency, Silk Touch, Fortune, Unbreaking, Looting, Fire Aspect, Knockback,
the bow's four, Thorns, Respiration, Aqua Affinity, Feather Falling and
Mending.

`engine/potions.ts` lists the potions (night vision, invisibility, fire
resistance, swiftness, slowness, healing, harming, poison, regeneration,
strength, weakness and water breathing, each with long and strong forms where
the original has them) and what brews into what; `engine/brewing.ts` runs the
stand: blaze powder for twenty brews, twenty seconds a brew. Each variant is its
own item, and each has a splash twin that bursts on whatever it hits and reaches
everything within four blocks. Healing hurts the undead and harming heals them.
Cauldrons hold three bottles of water, and hoppers feed brewing stands.
Blaze powder and nether wart come from the Nether.

## Rails, minecarts and boats

`engine/rails.ts` gives rails their ten shapes: two straights, four slopes and
four curves. A rail placed by a player joins the track around it, and the ends
it meets swing round to it. Powered rails boost a moving cart, kick a stopped one
away from a wall, and brake it when unpowered. Power runs along a line of them
for eight rails. Detector rails give a signal while a cart is on them, and
activator rails light TNT carts and throw riders out.

`engine/vehicles.ts` has the minecart, the TNT minecart and boats in five
woods. Carts run along the line between a rail's ends, a curve included, and top
out at eight blocks a second. Boats float, turn with A and D, and glide on ice.
Right-click to get in; sneak to get out. Online, whoever rides a vehicle drives
it on their own screen and reports where it went, so a guest's boat answers the
keys at once instead of a round trip later.

## Villages

`engine/villages.ts` plans at most one village per 20×20-chunk region, in
plains, desert, savanna, taiga and snowy plains, from the seed alone — so every
chunk of a village is built the same way whichever order the chunks generate
in. A village is a well and bell in a square, two to four roads out of it, and
houses along them: small and large houses, libraries and fenced farms, in the
biome's own wood or sandstone. Trees are kept off the houses and roads, and the
roads bridge water with planks.

The first time the well's chunk generates, the host settles it: a villager for
each house at its work station and an iron golem at the well; chest loot goes in
as each chunk arrives. `meta.villages` records which villages are settled, so
reloading never doubles them.

A villager takes up the trade of a free work station within eight blocks —
composter (farmer), lectern (librarian), smoker (butcher), barrel (fisherman),
fletching table, loom (shepherd), stonecutter (mason), smithing table
(toolsmith), brewing stand (cleric) or cauldron (leatherworker). One never
traded with loses its trade if its station is broken. Right-click to trade
(`engine/trading.ts`): trades earn it experience and it gains two new offers at
each of five levels; offers restock twice a day. Librarians sell enchanted books,
Mending included.

Zombies with no player to chase go after villagers, who run. Iron golems fight
monsters (never creepers) and anyone who hits them; build one from four iron
blocks in a T with a carved pumpkin on top. `/summon villager librarian` takes a
trade; online, a guest's trades go to the host, which checks the offer is still
open before it counts.

## Dimensions and the Nether

A world is one seed and three dimensions (`engine/dimension.ts`). Only the
dimension the player is in is loaded: going through a portal saves the one
being left (its changed chunks and its entities, into `meta.otherEntities`),
and `Game.changeDimension` builds the next — its world, generator, workers,
block rules, redstone and streamer — while the renderer, audio, player and
network link carry across. Chunks are stored per dimension (`n:` and `e:` key
prefixes under the world's own key range, so deleting and exporting a world
still takes everything). Dying outside the overworld respawns in it.

`engine/nether.ts` generates the Nether: caverns from 3D noise on a coarse
grid, bedrock above and below, a lava sea at y=31, and five biomes picked by
the nearest point in two climate noises — nether wastes, soul sand valleys
(soul fire, basalt pillars, fossils), crimson and warped forests (nylium, huge
fungi, vines) and basalt deltas. Glowstone hangs from the ceilings; quartz and
gold ore run through the netherrack and ancient debris hides deep where no air
touches it. Fortresses — bridges on legs over the lava, corridors, blaze
spawner platforms and nether wart gardens with a chest — are planned per
12×12-chunk region like villages; `/locate fortress` finds the nearest.

Portals (`engine/portal.ts`): fire lit inside an obsidian frame (2–21 wide,
3–21 tall, corners optional) fills it on its first tick, whoever lit it; a
broken frame lets the sheet go. Four seconds in a portal (at once in creative)
crosses over, eight overworld blocks to one Nether block. Every lit portal is
recorded in `meta.portals`, so a trip comes out of the nearest one within 128
blocks (16 in the Nether) and only builds a new one — on the nearest standing
room, or on an obsidian ledge cut into the rock — when there is none.

The Nether's rules: water boils away, beds explode, lava runs three times as
fast and twice as far, fire burns forever on netherrack, magma burns the feet,
soul sand slows. Its mobs spawn by biome whatever the light: zombified piglins
(neutral until one is struck — then all of them), ghasts (fireballs that can be
batted back), magma cubes, piglins (hostile unless you wear gold; hand them a
gold ingot to barter), hoglins, and in fortresses blazes and wither skeletons
(whose blow withers). Netherite comes from smelting ancient debris into scrap,
four scraps and four gold make an ingot, and a smithing table turns diamond
gear into netherite, enchantments and all.

Online, the party travels together: when the host goes through, every guest
follows (`dm`), waits until the host has said which chunks of the new dimension
it changed (`mk`) and where it came out (`dp`), and lands beside it. A guest
stepping into a portal alone is told that the host leads the way.

## The End

Strongholds (`engine/stronghold.ts`) lie in rings around the world's centre —
three between 640 and 1152 blocks out, more in each ring beyond — planned in
their own coordinates (portal room at the origin) and turned a random quarter.
Halls lead from the portal room to a hub, a library, a storeroom, a prison and
a fountain room, and up stairs toward the surface. The portal room holds twelve
end portal frames on a dais over lava (a tenth of them already holding an eye)
and a silverfish spawner. An eye of ender — an ender pearl and blaze powder —
thrown in the overworld flies twelve blocks toward the nearest portal room and
drops back (one in five shatters); `/locate stronghold` gives it straight away.
Set in a frame, the twelfth eye fills the ring with portal.

`engine/end.ts` generates the End on the original's plan: a height value per
column — a hundred at the centre falling off with distance, and past 1024
blocks of void a scatter of outer islands, each with its own fall-off — decides
whether there is land, how thick it is (flat tops, hanging cones) and the biome:
Highlands (hills and chorus forests, End cities), Midlands, Barrens, and the
small islands. The main island has ten obsidian spikes in a ring 42 out (two
caged in iron bars), each topped with bedrock and an end crystal, and the exit
portal's bedrock basin at its centre. Arrival is on a five-by-five obsidian
platform at (100, 48, 0), built fresh each time. The End has no sun: a fixed
half-light, distant fog, and endermen everywhere.

The fight (`engine/dragon.ts`, `game/endFight.ts`) starts on the first arrival:
the dragon circles the spikes, strafes players with fireballs that leave a
lingering cloud of breath, charges, and — the likelier the fewer crystals are
left — perches on the portal's pillar and breathes on whoever is near. A
crystal within 32 blocks heals it (the pink beam); destroying the one it draws
on hurts it. Perched it shrugs off arrows; it breaks every block it flies into
except end stone, obsidian, bedrock and iron bars. When it dies it rises for ten
seconds, then the exit portal lights, the dragon egg is laid on the pillar (the
first time — it flees a touch) and a gateway opens 96 out. A gateway (reached
by an ender pearl, or on elytra) flings a player to its far end over the outer
islands, which the generator always gives land. Four end crystals on the
portal's rim summon the dragon again. The boss bar shows its health.

End cities stand on the highlands: a purpur house, a tower with a ladder, a
lookout, and often a ship moored alongside whose chest always holds elytra.
Worn in the chest slot, elytra open with a jump in mid-fall and glide by the
original's formula (a dive trades height for speed, a climb spends it; hitting a
wall hurts); a firework rocket pushes a glider along. Chorus fruit teleports its
eater; popped, it makes purpur. Endermen are neutral until looked in the eye
(or struck) — a carved pumpkin on the head hides the look — slip every arrow by
teleporting, flee water, and carry blocks about.

Online, the party travels to the End and home together as through the Nether;
gateways and pearls move only the player who used them, and the dragon's
death is everyone's advancement.

## Commands

With cheats on (a world option) or in creative: `/time`, `/gamemode`, `/give`,
`/tp`, `/weather`, `/summon`, `/effect`, `/xp`, `/setblock`, `/fill`, `/kill`,
`/clear`, `/spawnpoint`, `/difficulty`, `/gamerule`, `/enchant`; `/summon slime 4`
takes a size and `/summon villager mason` a trade;
`/locate village|fortress|stronghold|end_city` finds the nearest one, and
`/dimension overworld|nether|end` crosses over as if through a portal; `/seed` and `/help` for
everyone. An online guest cannot run the ones that change the shared world.

## Saving

Worlds live in IndexedDB — only chunks that were changed are stored, gzipped.
The game saves every 30 seconds, when the tab is hidden, and on quit. If
IndexedDB is unavailable (some private modes) the game says so on screen and
keeps the world in memory; export it to keep it.

The world list exports a world to a `.blockcraft.json` file and imports one
back. **Upload to Cloud** stores that same file in `craft_worlds`, owner-only
under RLS; **Cloud Worlds…** lists and downloads them on any device signed in
to the same account. The table arrives with `supabase db push` — until then
the cloud buttons say exactly that, and nothing else is affected.

## Multiplayer

The host owns the world. Esc → **Open to friends** gives a six-letter code;
friends use **Multiplayer**, type it and join. "Online" goes through Supabase
Realtime broadcast channels (`blockcraft:<code>`); "Tabs on this device" uses a
BroadcastChannel and needs no network at all.

Guests generate untouched terrain from the seed themselves and fetch only
chunks the host reports as modified. Block edits are applied at once where they
are made, sent to the host, numbered and rebroadcast, so every client converges
on the host's order; edits for a chunk still loading are held and replayed
after it arrives. Mobs, water, time and weather run only on the host. A host
keeps ticking in a background tab (browsers stop animation frames there), and
a guest's inventory is stored in the host's world under the guest's
browser-stable id, so it is still there when they rejoin.

## Checking a change

```
npx vitest run src/test/craft-*      # engine, gameplay, input rules, cloud saves
npx tsc --noEmit -p tsconfig.app.json
```

In a development build the running game is `window.__blockcraft`, which is
how the browser checks during development read positions and screens.
